How Brightline Builds Your Production Estimate

Your production estimate is generated at the time of system design using a combination of your roof's pitch, azimuth, and shading profile, your local historical solar irradiance data (sourced from a 20-year weather dataset), and the rated output of your specific panel and inverter models. The result is a modeled annual figure—for most systems, within ±8% of actual output under typical conditions.

Common Reasons for a Wider Gap

  • Weather variability: A cloudier-than-average year in your area can reduce generation by 5–12% on its own.
  • Shading changes: Tree growth, new structures, or seasonal shadows not present during your site survey.
  • System downtime: Inverter faults, microinverter outages, or grid outages reduce cumulative output. Check the Alerts tab in Brightline Home for any recorded events.
  • Panel soiling: Heavy pollen seasons or bird activity can cut output 2–4% if panels haven't been cleaned.
  • Temperature extremes: Panels lose roughly 0.35% efficiency per °C above 25°C (77°F), so unusually hot summers affect totals.

What to Do If the Gap Seems Too Large

If your actual output is more than 10% below your estimate after accounting for the factors above, contact Brightline Support to request a Performance Variance Review. A technician will audit your monitoring data and flag any hardware issues. You can also review your full estimate breakdown in the app—see How to view and download your production estimate report for step-by-step instructions.
