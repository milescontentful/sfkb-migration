Why Estimates and Actuals Sometimes Diverge

Production estimates are calculated at the time of your site survey using historical solar irradiance data, your roof's pitch and azimuth, shading analysis, and the rated output of your specific panel model. They represent a long-run average under typical conditions — not a guaranteed figure for any single year.

Common Causes of a Lower-Than-Expected Output

  • Unusual weather: A year with significantly more cloud cover, rain, or wildfire smoke than the 20-year historical average used in your estimate will reduce generation.
  • Panel soiling: Dust, pollen, bird droppings, or debris can reduce output by 3–8% if panels have not been cleaned in 12+ months.
  • Shading changes: Tree growth or new structures near your home may create shading that did not exist during your site survey.
  • Inverter or microinverter underperformance: One or more underperforming units can drag total output down. Check the Device Status tab in Brightline Home for any flagged units.
  • System degradation: Panels degrade at roughly 0.5% per year, which is already factored into multi-year estimates but can appear as a small annual shortfall.

When to Contact Support

A gap of up to 10% below the annual estimate is generally within normal variance. If your shortfall exceeds 15% and weather alone does not explain it, contact Brightline Support — a performance review ticket can be opened from Reports → Production Estimate → Request Performance Review in Brightline Home.
